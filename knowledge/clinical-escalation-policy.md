# Clinical Escalation Policy

**Organization:** Northstar Community Care
**Policy ID:** CLN-ESC-310
**Effective date:** February 1, 2026
**Document owner:** Clinical Quality Department

## Purpose

This policy describes how staff should escalate operationally significant concerns identified during a home visit. It does not provide treatment instructions and does not replace professional judgment, emergency protocols, or organizational clinical policies.

## Priority levels

### Low priority

Low-priority situations are issues that do not appear to require immediate action but should be documented and communicated through normal channels.

Examples include:

* A non-urgent scheduling concern.
* A request for additional education.
* A routine supply question.
* A minor workflow issue that does not prevent completion of the visit.

The clinician should document the concern and notify the appropriate team member by the end of the working day.

### Medium priority

Medium-priority situations may affect service delivery or require timely supervisor review.

Examples include:

* A repeated inability to contact a patient.
* A change in caregiver availability.
* A documentation issue that prevents completion of a required workflow.
* A concern requiring review before the next scheduled visit.

The clinician should contact the clinical supervisor within **one hour** and record the notification method and time.

### High priority

High-priority situations involve an immediate safety concern, a potentially serious change, or circumstances in which waiting for a routine response may be inappropriate.

The clinician must follow the organization’s emergency procedure, use professional judgment, and contact emergency services when required. Emergency procedures take precedence over completing documentation in the mobile application.

## Supervisor escalation

When contacting a supervisor, the clinician should provide:

* Their name and role.
* The general nature of the concern.
* The time the concern was identified.
* Actions already taken.
* Whether emergency services or another authorized party was contacted.

## AI assistant restrictions

An AI assistant must never:

* Diagnose a patient.
* Determine the appropriate treatment.
* Decide whether emergency services are necessary.
* Replace the clinician’s judgment.
* Change a care plan.
* Assign a final priority level for a real patient.

The assistant may summarize this fictional policy and direct users to authorized personnel.

## Required documentation

After an escalation, the clinician must record the observed concern, notifications made, instructions received, actions taken, and follow-up plan. Documentation should distinguish direct observations from information reported by another person.

If the application is unavailable, the clinician should follow the approved downtime process and enter the documentation when access is restored.
